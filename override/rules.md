# OVERRIDE

A game of rival factions, nerve, and the occasional all-out war. For one to three players, using a standard 52-card deck with the jokers removed.

These are the rules as they'd read in the box. The player-facing text comes first, written for a small booklet or a two-sided reference card. The precise rulings at the end settle the rare corners without cluttering the main rules.

## The game (3 players)

### Object

The object of the game is to capture more cards than your opponents. Win a clash and you keep the cards in it. When the deck runs out, the biggest pile wins.

### The factions

Three of the suits are rival factions, and each one has the measure of another. Intelligence outsmarts Strength, Strength overpowers Speed, and Speed outruns Intelligence. The fourth suit, Ingenuity, is a band of mercenaries: it belongs to no faction and never wins a faction matchup, but it fights hard on numbers alone.

### Card values

Cards run from low to high. Ace is 1, the number cards are worth their face value, and then come Bronze (Jack) at 11, Silver (Queen) at 12, and Gold (King) at 13.

### Setup

Each player claims a different faction as their Home: Intelligence, Strength, or Speed. No one may claim Ingenuity. Shuffle the deck, deal five cards to each player, and place the rest face down in the middle as the draw pile.

### Playing a clash

Each round is a clash. Everyone picks a card in secret, places it face down, and then all of you flip at once. Work out who wins, and the winner gathers every card played into their score pile. Then everyone draws back up to five, and the next clash begins.

### Winning a clash

Look at the factions on the table, and set any Ingenuity cards aside for the moment.

If exactly two factions are present and one beats the other, that faction takes the clash no matter the numbers. Say Strength meets Speed: Strength wins, even if Speed played a Gold and Strength played a 2. If more than one player played the winning faction, the higher card among them takes it.

Otherwise the factions cancel out, and the highest card on the whole table wins, Ingenuity included. That is how a mercenary steals a clash, and how one high card carries the day when all three factions turn up at once.

### Home advantage

A card of your own Home faction counts three ranks higher whenever a clash comes down to numbers, so your Home 8 fights as an 11 and your Gold fights as a 16. This never overturns a faction matchup: a faction that beats another always wins, however high the losing card. And each time you win a clash with a Home card, take the top card of the draw pile straight into your score pile as a bonus.

### War

Sometimes the highest cards tie. The tied players then go to war, and everyone else drops out, leaving their cards in the pot. Each player in the war sets one card face down and reveals a second, and you settle those revealed cards the usual way. The winner takes the whole pot; if they tie again, fight another war.

If you are caught short, do what you can: with one card left, just reveal it, and with none, you fall out and leave your cards behind. The last player still fighting wins the pot.

### Ending the game

When the draw pile is gone, keep playing until your hands are empty. Then count your piles. Most cards wins, and if two players tie, they share the victory.

## Playing with one or two

OVERRIDE is built for three factions at the table, so with fewer players you fill the empty chairs with Bots. A Bot holds a hand of five like anyone else, but it has no strategy: each clash, shuffle its hand, turn one card up at random, and then deal it back to five. Everything in the main rules still applies, Home advantage and war included.

### Two players

You and your opponent each claim a Home faction. The third faction goes to a single Bot, which plays that leftover suit as its Home and earns its bonuses just as you do. Deal five cards to each of you and to the Bot, and play as normal. The largest pile still wins.

### Solo

Claim one Home faction for yourself and hand the other two to Bots. Deal five to yourself and to each Bot, then play through the deck as usual. At the end, compare piles: beat both Bots and the run is yours.

## Precise rulings

These settle the rare cases. They don't change anything above; they only pin down the corners.

- **Counting factions.** Judge a clash by how many of the three real factions are on the table, ignoring Ingenuity. Exactly two means a faction matchup. Zero, one, or all three means the highest card wins.
- **A tie within the winning faction.** When two players share the winning faction and their cards tie after Home advantage, they go to war, exactly as a tie for highest card does.
- **Home advantage only settles numbers.** The three-rank bonus applies to every comparison of card values, in a highest-card clash and in a war, and never to a faction matchup.
- **A war that empties every hand.** If all the tied players run out of cards at once, split the pot as evenly as you can; any odd card goes to the player whose last revealed card was highest.
- **Leftovers at the end.** If the draw pile is gone and only one player still holds cards, there is no one to clash against, so those cards are set aside and don't count.
- **You must play.** While you hold cards, you play one into every clash; you can't sit one out.
