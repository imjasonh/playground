# Clash

A game of timing and held nerve for two to four players. Every clash a pot of
cards gathers on the table, and everyone plays for it in secret, all at once:
one card, face down, from a small hand of four. The card you play is spent for
good, win or lose — but the pot you win slides back under your pile to come
round again. Your pile is your stock of cards, and when the first player runs
out, whoever holds the most has won. So the real question is never "can I win
this one?" but "is this pot worth spending a good card on?"

These are the rules as they'd read in the box: the player-facing text first,
written for a small booklet or a two-sided card, then a few precise rulings that
settle the rare corners.

## What you need

A Clash deck is a standard 52-card deck with the four Aces removed, which leaves
48 cards. The four suits are the factions: Intelligence, Strength, Speed, and
Ingenuity. No faction beats another; a suit matters only as your Home and for
the small bonus it brings.

The Aces aren't dead weight. Each one marks a Home. At the start, every player
takes the Ace of one faction, either by choice or dealt at random, and keeps it
face up in front of them so everyone can see who owns what. Aces for factions no
one took go back in the box.

## Card values

Cards are worth the number they show. The picture cards carry on from ten:
Bronze (Jack) is 11, Silver (Queen) is 12, and Gold (King) is 13.

## Setup

Give each player the Ace that marks their Home. Shuffle the other 48 cards and
deal them out evenly, so everyone holds the same size stack: 24 cards at two
players, 16 at three, 12 at four.

Take four cards off your stack into your **hand**, which you look at and keep
secret. The rest stays face down in front of you as your **pile**. The pile does
double duty: it feeds the pot at the start of each clash and refills your hand
at the end of one. It's also your stock for the game — when both your pile and
your hand are gone, you're out, and that ends it for everyone.

## Playing a clash

Each round is a clash. First, everyone turns the top card of their pile face up
into the middle. Together these cards are the **pot**, and winning it is the
point of the clash.

Now everyone bids. Look at the pot, then choose one card from your hand and
place it face down. When everyone is ready, reveal together.

Work out each player's bid; the highest takes the pot and slides those cards
face down under their own pile, to come round again. The card you played, by
contrast, is spent whether you win or lose: put it aside, out of the game for
good. You win the pot, never your own card back.

Then everyone draws from the top of their pile to fill their hand back to four,
and the next clash begins.

## Working out a bid

Your bid is the number on the card you played. Then you add your Home bonus: one
for every card of your own faction sitting in the pot, and one more when the
card you played is your own faction.

Say your Home is Speed. The pot holds a Speed 9 and an Intelligence 4, so it's
worth 13, and you've already earned one from the Speed card waiting there. You
play a Speed 7: that's 7 in numbers, plus one more for playing a Speed card, so
you bid 7 + 1 (the pot) + 1 (your card) = 9.

## Winning the clash

The highest bid wins. If the top two bids tie, no one wins the pot this round;
leave those cards in the middle, and they join the next clash's pot on top of
the fresh cards. A clash can carry more than once, and the pot grows each time.

## Ending the game

Every clash you spend one card for good, so the piles only shrink — sooner or
later someone runs dry. The game ends the moment a player can't play: their hand
is empty and their pile has nothing left to refill it. If only your pile runs
out, keep playing the cards still in your hand, and simply add nothing to the
pot.

When the game ends, everyone counts the cards they still hold, hand and pile
together. The most cards wins. If two players tie, they share the victory.

## Filling seats, and playing alone

Clash wants two to four players, and you fill any empty chairs with Bots. You
can play a whole game solo against Bots this way, or round out a three-handed
table to four.

A Bot takes a Home and a full deal like anyone else, but you never hold or
manage its cards. A Bot has no use for a hand you'd look at, so it keeps its
whole stack face down as one **pile**, the way everyone's pile is.

Its whole rule is a pair of flips. Each clash, the Bot turns the top card of its
pile into the pot like everyone else. Then, for its bid, it turns the next card
of its pile and plays it. That card is its bid, and it's spent like any other;
cards the Bot wins go face down under its pile to come round again.

That's the whole of it. You never judge a pot's worth for the Bot and you never
choose a card for it; you flip from the top. A flipped card is as likely to be
high as low, so the Bot takes its share of pots by luck while you save your
strong cards for the pots worth winning — enough to keep it honest, and simple
enough to run while you think about your own hand. Because the Bot only ever
flips its own cards, it plays the same whether it's the one empty chair or three
of them.

## Precise rulings

These settle the rare cases. They change nothing above.

- **One card, always.** Each clash you play exactly one card from your hand —
  there's no passing, and no playing two. You play on even after your pile is
  spent, straight from your hand, until that too is empty.
- **The Home bonus counts faction, not Home.** You add one for each card of your
  own faction, wherever it is in the pot, no matter who anted it.
- **Winnings go to the bottom.** Cards you win are slipped under your pile, so
  they come back into play only after the cards already above them.
- **A tie on the last clash.** If the clash that ends the game ties, there's no
  round left to carry into, so leave that pot in the middle; it counts for no
  one.
- **Spent cards are gone.** The card you play is never won back and never
  re-enters your hand, win or lose.
- **A tie for the most cards.** If two or more players end level on cards, they
  share the win.
