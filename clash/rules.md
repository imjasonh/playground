# Clash

A game of tight budgets and held nerve for two to four players. Every round a
prize gathers on the table and everyone bids for it in secret, all at once. You
bid with cards from a hand that never refills, so the real question is never
"can I win this one?" but "is this the one worth spending on?"

These are the rules as they'd read in the box: the player-facing text first,
written for a small booklet or a two-sided card, then a few precise rulings that
settle the rare corners.

## What you need

A Clash deck is a standard 52-card deck with the four Aces removed, which leaves
48 cards. The four suits are the factions: Intelligence, Strength, Speed, and
Ingenuity. No faction beats another; a suit matters only as your Home and for
the small bonus it brings.

The Aces aren't dead weight. Each one marks a Home. At the start, every player
takes the Ace of one faction, either by choice or dealt at random, and keeps it
face up in front of them so everyone can see who owns what. Aces for factions no
one took go back in the box.

## Card values

Cards are worth the number they show. The picture cards carry on from ten:
Bronze (Jack) is 11, Silver (Queen) is 12, and Gold (King) is 13.

## Setup

Give each player the Ace that marks their Home. Shuffle the other 48 cards and
deal each player two face-down stacks: a **hand**, which you look at and keep
secret, and an **ante pile**, which stays face down until it's spent. Deal the
sizes for your table from this list, and you'll use the whole deck:

| Players | Hand | Ante pile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 15 | 9 |
| 3 | 10 | 6 |
| 4 | 7 | 5 |

Your hand is your whole budget for the game. You never draw more, so spend it
with that in mind.

## Playing a clash

Each round is a clash. First, everyone turns the top card of their ante pile
face up into the middle. Together these cards are the **pot**, and the prize is
the total of their numbers.

Now everyone bids. Look at the pot, then choose any number of cards from your
hand, as many or as few as you like, and place them face down. When everyone is
ready, reveal together.

Work out each player's bid and the highest takes the pot: set those cards in
front of you as your winnings. Here is the part that makes a budget hurt. The
cards you bid are spent whether you win or lose. Put them aside, out of the
game. You win the pot, never your own bid back.

Then the next clash begins. When the ante piles are empty, the game is over.

## Working out a bid

Your bid is the total of the numbers on the cards you played. Then you add your
Home bonus: one for every card of your own faction sitting in the pot, and one
for every card of your own faction you just played.

Say your Home is Speed. The pot holds a Speed 9 and an Intelligence 4, so it's
worth 13, and you've already earned one from the Speed card waiting there. You
play a Speed 7 and a Strength 6: that's 13 in numbers, plus one more for your
Speed card, so you bid 13 + 1 (the pot) + 1 (your card) = 15.

## Winning the clash

The highest bid wins. If the top two bids tie, no one wins the pot this round;
leave those cards where they are, and they join the next clash's pot on top of
the fresh antes. A clash can carry more than once, and the pot grows each time.

## Ending the game

When everyone's ante pile has run out, add up the numbers on the cards you won.
The biggest total wins. If two players tie, they share the victory.

## Filling seats, and playing alone

Clash wants two to four players, and you fill any empty chairs with Bots. You
can play a whole game solo against Bots this way, or round out a three-handed
table to four.

A Bot takes a Home and a full deal like anyone else, and it plays by one rule:
it commits its highest card, and a second-highest as well whenever the pot shows
its own faction. That's the whole of it. You never judge a pot's worth for the
Bot; you glance at the suits in the pot and play one card, or two when one of
them is the Bot's. It fights a little harder for the pots that carry its colors,
which is enough to keep it honest and simple enough to run while you think about
your own hand.

## Precise rulings

These settle the rare cases. They change nothing above.

- **You may pass.** Playing no cards is a legal bid. Your bid is then just your
  Home bonus for your faction's cards in the pot, which is often zero.
- **Winning with an empty bid.** If everyone passes, the highest bid can be a
  Home bonus alone, and that player wins the pot. If those are tied too, the pot
  carries like any other tie.
- **The Home bonus counts faction, not Home.** You add one for each card of your
  own faction, wherever it is in the pot, no matter who anted it.
- **A tie on the last clash.** If the final clash ties, there's no round left to
  carry into, so set that pot aside; it counts for no one.
- **Spent cards are gone.** Cards you bid are never won back and never re-enter
  your hand, win or lose.
