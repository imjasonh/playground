# Verified ESP32-C3 bootloader: working status

This file is the detailed status of the verified OTA bootloader work in
[pull request #710](https://github.com/imjasonh/playground/pull/710). The
pull request description has the current summary and burndown. This file
keeps the detail that the description used to carry: phase-by-phase
status, decisions, release blockers, known gaps, and a map of the files.
Sections dated Nov 16 haven't been rewritten since; where they disagree
with the description, the description is current.

## Function-level proofs

A function counts as proved when a correctness theorem covers all of its
compiled instructions in the current image, as listed in `PROVED` in
[`c3-boot-proofs/scripts/coverage.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/coverage.py).
On Nov 19, 2026, 260 of the 263 functions that `boot_main` can reach were
proved: 22,594 of their 28,513 instructions. The three left are
`c3_verify::sigstore::verify`, `boot_main`, and `boot_main`'s slot-check
closure.

`sigstore::verify` is proved in stages, one Lean file per stretch of its
machine code (`c3-boot-proofs/C3BootProofs/Proofs/Sv*.lean`), generated by
[`c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sigstore-verify.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sigstore-verify.py).
Each stage proves that its stretch either fails with `SvErrRet` or reaches
its end with the frame, registers, and saved state its successor needs.
The stages cover all of the function's instructions, including the
inlined P-256 ECDSA check at offsets 0xFBA to 0x2034:

- `SvEcA*`: the range checks and the key decode.
- `SvIv*` and `SvEcDs`: the inversion of `s`.
- `SvEcD`: `u₁` and `u₂`.
- `SvEcT*` and `SvEcW*`: the tables of odd multiples and the wNAF digits.
- `SvEcMl*`: the main loop of the double-scalar multiplication, with its
  loop induction against the model `mlMain2`.
- `SvZ*`: the inversion of `Z`. Its constant-time divstep loop (`SvZDs*`)
  is proved against the same `dsStep` model as `p384_verify_prehash`'s.
- `SvZE`: the affine `x` and the comparison with `r`.

The batch loops of the two inversions are proved by induction, the
counterpart of `Pv384HLoop.lean`: `zv_loop` against the model `zvLoop`
for `Z` (`SvZLoop2.lean`), and `iv_loop` and `iv_exit` against `ivLoop8`
for `s` (`SvIvLoop.lean`), which also stops when `g` reaches zero.
[`gen-sv-zloop.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-zloop.py) and
[`gen-sv-ivloop.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-ivloop.py) write
them from the stages' statements.

`sv_p256` (`SvP256All.lean`) composes the whole check from 0xFBA to
0x2034 out of four segments (`SvP256A.lean` to `SvP256D.lean`) and the
three loops. The scripts that write them use
[`svchain.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/svchain.py), which passes each
stage the previous stage's facts by name. It ends in one of three ways:
- An error return, when `r` or `s` is out of range, the key isn't on the
  curve, or the affine `x` of `u₁G + u₂Q` doesn't match `r`.
- A panic, when the inversion of `s` doesn't end with `f = ±1`, or when
  the main loop reads an invalid digit.
- 0x2034, with the frame and registers that the rest of the function
  needs.

Its statement doesn't mention any intermediate machine. The two inverses
are witnesses that `sgnVal` ties to the states of `ivLoop8` and `zvLoop`.
It derives the bounds on the constants it reads (p, n, the base point,
1 in Montgomery form, and the identity's coordinate) from the image's
data, which `BootloaderData5.lean` extracts. It still takes one
mathematical hypothesis: the range bound of the inversion of `Z`
(`hzi`), which is safegcd's bound on `d` and `e`.

### Composing the rest of `sigstore::verify`

Every stage also states the frame ranges it may write
(`SvKeepL B n0.mem n'.mem [...]`), so a composed theorem can carry a frame
fact past stages that don't mention it. Three scripts compose stages:

- [`gen-sv-segs.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-segs.py) writes
  straight runs of stages with
  [`svseg.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/svseg.py). A segment ends in an
  error return, a panic, or every stage's success condition followed by
  the state at its end. It derives each slice hypothesis (`SvIn`) from the
  parse that found the slice. It takes a frame fact that no stage in the
  segment states as a hypothesis about the segment's start. Two stages
  that start at the same place under a hypothesis and its negation become
  one merged stage ([`svmerge.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/svmerge.py)).
  Values the two leave differently become witnesses.
- [`gen-sv-loop.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-loop.py) writes the
  theorem for `k` iterations of a stage that returns to its own start. The
  loop's variables are a tuple that one step function updates
  (`svIter`).
- [`gen-sv-search.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-search.py) writes
  the searches through the root's lists: an error return, or the first
  entry that matches and the end of the stage that handles it.

A segment's statement has the same form as a stage's, so segments,
merged stages, and the `_st` forms of loops and searches compose into
larger segments. A merge decides between its stages on a hypothesis and
its negation, on `a < b` against `b ≤ a`, or on whether a decode
returned `none`. [`gen-sv-attrloop.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-attrloop.py)
writes the one loop so far whose step depends on what it parses: the
signed attributes, as a chain of `sv_attrStep` relations (`svChain`).

Composed so far:

- Segments: the leaf, its extensions, the intermediate that issued it, and
  the leaf's signature, from the entry at 0x72 to the policy check at 0xDE6
  (`sv_seg_cert`); the policy and the signature's DER (`sv_seg_dpv`); the
  timestamp up to its signed attributes (`sv_seg_tsa`); the `TSTInfo` and
  the timestamp's signature (`sv_seg_tst`); the checkpoint's origin and size
  (`sv_seg_ck`); and the entry's index and the validity windows up to the
  successful exit (`sv_seg_win`).
- Loops: the leaf's extensions (`sv_extloop`, with the OIDs of an
  `ExtKeyUsage` value in `sv_ekuloop`), the signed attributes
  (`sv_attrloop`), the size's digits (`sv_ckdloop_run`), the checkpoint's
  signature lines (`sv_cosigloop`), and a cosignature's base64 groups with
  the end of its line (`sv_cgloop_st`).
- Searches: the issuer, the TSA, the log, and the space in a signature
  line (`sv_isearch`, `sv_tsearch`, `sv_csearch`, `sv_nsearch`).

[`gen-sv-relloop.py`](../c3-boot-proofs/scripts/gen-sv-relloop.py) writes
the loops whose step depends on what one pass parses (the extensions, the
`ExtKeyUsage` OIDs, the signature lines). `sv_lh` restates the leaf's hash
(`sv_lhash`) and the `p384_verify_prehash` call (`sv_lpv`) as a stage. It
states the call's result over any memory that holds the key, the prehash,
`r`, and `s` (`SvPvRes`), since `pvModel` reads only those
(`pvModel_congr`).

Left: the identity-policy slots as slices through the extensions loop, so
the policy check composes after `sv_seg_cert`; `sv_p256`, `sv_tpv`, and the
successful return as stages; one theorem from the entry to the return;
the layout of `Bundle` and `SigstoreRoot` in memory, and that theorem
against `sigstoreVerify` in `Spec/Sigstore.lean`; and the models
(`ivLoop8`, `mlMain2`, `zvLoop`, `pvModel`) against `p256.verify` and
P-384 in `Spec/Ecdsa.lean`.

`_start` and the clock and watchdog `setup` that runs before `boot_main`
aren't among the extracted functions, so they are trusted.

## What was proved by Nov 16, in detail

Individual functions, each against its specification: the parsers for the slot manifest and the firmware image header, the CRC check, `memset` and `memcpy` for any buffers, the P-256 elliptic-curve arithmetic under ECDSA signature checks, SHA-256 of any byte string, from the compression function up through `update`, `finalize`, and the one-shot `sha256` that the timestamp checks call, and P-384's field multiplication, the single function a boot spends the most time in. On Nov 11 the build changed to make the image easier to prove: no LLVM machine outliner, `sha2`'s loop-based SHA-256 and SHA-512, and the Merkle check as its own function. The image shrank from 133 KB to 89 KB, and the proofs are being redone for it. The security properties themselves (no rollback, only signed firmware boots, the trust root only moves forward) are proved against a Lean model of the boot logic, not yet against the compiled code.

The signature checks as a whole (ECDSA P-256 and P-384, Ed25519), the certificate and Sigstore parsing, and the final theorem: that the shipped binary, from power-on, only ever starts firmware that passes every check. The Sigstore check now has a Lean specification: `sigstoreVerify` in `Spec/Sigstore.lean` decides what `c3_verify::sigstore::verify` decides, with ECDSA, Ed25519, and SHA-384 specified on natural numbers, and it agrees with the Rust code on every Sigstore conformance case and 32,000 mutations. What's left is proving that the compiled `sigstore::verify` computes it.

## Size and speed

The proofs don't change the code, so they cost nothing on the device. The bootloader is about 89 KB in a 160 KiB flash area, and each firmware slot has 1,920 KiB of the 4 MB flash. On the processor model, one full update check runs about 137 million instructions, at least 0.86 seconds at the chip's 160 MHz; a board has to measure the real time. About half of that time is P-384 arithmetic, for the timestamp authority's signatures. [`c3-boot/docs/measurements.md`](../c3-boot/docs/measurements.md) tracks size and speed per crate.

### Safe to test on boards

Nothing a test does should stop a board from taking updates, so:
- The bootloader burns no eFuses unless it was built with `C3_EFUSE_BURN=on`. Without that setting, the eFuse programming code isn't compiled in. The bootloader logs the raise it would have made and boots the slot.
- A development-key build (`C3_GENESIS=dev`) with burns on doesn't halt on a failed raise. It boots the slot instead.
- It never enables Secure Boot or flash encryption, and never disables USB download mode.
- A production-key build fails unless `C3_EFUSE_BURN=on`, and keeps the plan's halt. The `c3_*` build flags can't come from `RUSTFLAGS`.

QEMU tests both settings, including a board whose `SECURE_VERSION` is write-disabled.

### Decisions

- Retired trust-root keys are destroyed, the genesis keys included after the first rotation. The device keeps no record of key sets, so surviving keys could still sign trust roots.
- Demo builds signed under the release identity are valid releases for now. Once a simple demo has been tested on hardware, devices will accept only released images.
- The RV32 model makes misaligned loads and stores trap, accepts only the FENCE encodings compilers emit, and leaves out SYSTEM instructions for now. Each choice can only make a proof harder, never let a false one through.
- Machine-code proofs are about the pinned image, the default test build with development keys. A build with other keys is covered only where its function bytes are the same.
- Three code changes that make proofs easier landed together on Nov 11, as one image change, right after P-384's squaring was proved: `root_from_inclusion` as its own function, `sha2`'s compact backend for SHA-256 and SHA-512 (one setting covers both), and no LLVM machine outliner (`c3-boot/.cargo/config.toml`). The image went from 133,392 to 89,296 bytes and a boot runs 2.2 percent more instructions. Proofs written against the earlier image are kept, about its bytes, until each is redone.
- The model treats SRAM's instruction-bus and data-bus addresses as separate memory, while the chip aliases them. Proofs hold on the chip as long as no store reaches code through the data-bus alias. The platform model will make that an explicit obligation.

### Release blockers

- The eFuse controller lock is a no-op until a board shows which protection unit can lock it.
- Production genesis keys and offline signing tooling don't exist yet.
- The release identity must stop signing demo builds, once the hardware demo passes.

### Gaps and feasibility

- Proofs about the current image hold 54.6 percent of its instructions (15,572 of 28,526) and 99.6 percent of the instructions one boot runs (`scripts/coverage.py`). Boot coverage weighs functions by how long they run, so field arithmetic dominates it; the functions that `sigstore::verify` reaches hold 24,870 instructions, of which 61.4 percent are proved, and the deciding functions (`sigstore::verify`, `p384_verify_prehash`, `ed25519_verify`, and `boot_main`) none. `coverage.py` now reports that figure, and `docs/throughput.md` explains why it is the one to watch. Before the image change, proofs held 14.5 percent and 79.6 percent of the earlier image. Boot coverage counts instructions in proved code; the callers' proofs still have to discharge each theorem's hypotheses. The throughput report now projects about 420,000 proof lines for the whole image. SHA-256's generated rounds cost 6.6 lines per instruction, against the 3 to 4 the first report assumed.
- No headline theorem is started. Every function proof assumes a stack and memory layout that `check_slot`'s proof must establish, and no proof yet ties a function to its caller in the boot path.
- The hardware is trusted and untested: the eFuse lock, ROM state, and MMU behavior need a board.
- The proofs are pinned to one image. A compiler or dependency change moves every function, and the generators and extraction redo only part of that work.
- The earlier image's `sha256Compress_correct` doesn't claim that `gp` is preserved, though the function never writes it. `kcCompress_correct`, for the current image, does.
- This PR is large. It stays one PR, by decision, and grows as the proofs do.
- Covering every call costs more than covering one caller: `memcpy`'s misaligned path took 20 proof lines per instruction, the most so far.
